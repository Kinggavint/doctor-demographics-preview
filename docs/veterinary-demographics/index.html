Why does location matter for a veterinary practice?

A veterinary practice lives or dies on the households around it. Since 1983, Doctor Demographics has written 10,000+ reports for doctors and other private practice owners, including veterinarians, who need to know whether a place can support the practice they want to build or buy. The report is not a generic demographic printout. It is hand-written for a veterinary practice, and it is followed by a live 20-minute consultation.

Households, not just raw population, are the starting point, because pets live in households. It also matters how fast those households are growing, how many of them own pets and how much they spend on them, and what they earn. A site can look busy on a map and still be a poor fit if nearby clinics already serve those households, or if people cannot see, park at, or reach the office easily.

That is why a veterinary location decision is different from a general dental or medical one. The questions we hear most often from veterinarians are practical: how many households per vet is healthy in this area, whether there is room for another general practice, and whether it is wiser to start from scratch or buy an existing practice. The report examines those questions for your site or your study area; it does not replace your own judgment, and we will not invent a one-size-fits-all ratio on this page.

Which demographic factors matter most for veterinary?

  • Number of households, and how fast the area is growing. We start with households around the site or across the counties you ask us to study, and whether that household base is growing or shrinking.
  • How many households own pets, and how much they spend on them. Pet ownership and pet spending tell us more about veterinary demand than population counts alone.
  • Household income. Income helps show whether households in the area can support the kind of practice you want to operate.
  • Households per vet clinic nearby. We look at how many households there are for the veterinary clinics already in the area, which is the comparison clients often have in mind when they ask if there is room for another practice.
  • Visibility, parking and easy access. A clinic that is hard to see, hard to park at, or hard to reach will struggle even in a strong household market.

Frequently asked questions

Do you write reports specifically for veterinary practices?

Yes. Every report is written for the client's specialty. On a veterinary report we look at the location factors listed on this page, households and growth, pet-owning households and what they spend, household income, households per vet clinic nearby, and visibility, parking and easy access, and we write the analysis for a veterinary practice, not a generic office.

Is there a call after the report?

Yes. Every report is hand-written and includes a live 20-minute follow-up call to go over the findings. When we email the finished report, we include a booking link to schedule that call. You can also reply to that delivery email and we will set a time. Spouses, partners and consultants are welcome on the call.

How many households per vet is healthy?

We do not publish a single national number as a rule of thumb on this page. In the report we look at households per vet clinic nearby, together with how fast the area is growing, household income, pet ownership and spending, and how easy the site is to see and reach. We then go over what those figures mean for your site or area on the 20-minute follow-up call.

Is there room for another general veterinary practice here?

That is a site-specific question, which is what the reports are for. A Site Viability Report studies a specific address, including nearby clinics and the households around the site. A Best Sites Report compares up to five counties when you have not yet picked an address. We will tell you what the data shows, including when the news is not what you hoped to hear.

Should I start from scratch or buy an existing practice?

We can study either path. Send us the address of a site you might build or lease, or the address of a practice you might buy, and say which situation you are in when you order. The Site Viability Report is the usual starting point when you already have an address. We will walk through the findings with you on the follow-up call so you can compare those options with the local household and clinic picture.

What if something in the report is wrong? Can I get a refund?

It is not the policy of Doctor Demographics to offer refunds on reports. The full Refunds and Errors Policy is in our Terms and Conditions. If something in a report is wrong, an address or a detail we missed, we correct it and send the report again. Errors of that kind are not a reason for a refund. If a location was misidentified at the time you ordered, it can be corrected at an additional cost.

Do you track insurance accepted in the area?

No. We do not track insurance offered or accepted in the region.
