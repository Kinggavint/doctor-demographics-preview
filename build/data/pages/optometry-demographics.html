Why does location matter for an optometry practice?

An optometry practice draws both from the people who live near the office and from the people who work there during the day. Since 1983, Doctor Demographics has written 10,000+ reports for private practice owners, including optometrists, who are choosing, buying or moving a location. Each report is hand-written for the specialty, and each one includes a live 20-minute follow-up call.

Age mix matters in a way it does not for every profession: children and adults 45 and older are the groups Doctor Demographics is asked to look at most carefully for optometry, along with household income and education. It also matters how many people there already are per optometrist in the area, including the retail optical chains that many clients worry about, and whether the site itself is a retail spot with good visibility and traffic.

The questions we hear most often are straightforward: can I compete with the big retail optical stores nearby, is a shopping center or a standalone building better, and how many people per optometrist does this area need. Those questions can only be answered for a specific site or study area. This page explains the factors we use. It does not invent a standard ratio or tell you that one building type always wins.

Which demographic factors matter most for optometry?

  • Total population and daytime (worker) population. We look at who lives in the area and who is there during the workday, because an optometry practice can draw from both.
  • Age mix, especially children and adults 45 and older. These are the age groups we are asked to pay close attention to when we write an optometry report.
  • Household income and education. Income and education help describe whether the area can support the practice you want to operate.
  • People per optometrist, including retail optical chains. We include the large retail optical stores in the competition picture, not only independent offices.
  • A retail spot with good visibility and traffic. The site itself — whether people can see it and whether there is traffic to it — is part of the location decision, not an afterthought.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a call after the report?

Can I compete with the big retail optical stores nearby?

That depends on the specific site and on the stores already there. In the report we count people per optometrist and we include retail optical chains in that picture, along with visibility and traffic at the site. We will go over what that means for your practice on the 20-minute follow-up call. We will not pretend a chain is not there, and we will not promise a result.

Is a shopping center or a standalone building better?

There is no single answer for every optometrist. We look at whether the site is a retail spot with good visibility and traffic, together with the population, daytime workers, age mix and income around it. Send us the address you are considering — a shopping-center suite or a standalone building — and we will study that site. If you are still choosing an area, a Best Sites Report can compare counties before you commit to a building type.

How many people per optometrist does an area need?

We do not publish a single national ratio as a rule on this page. In the report we look at people per optometrist in the area, including retail optical chains, alongside total population, daytime population, age mix, income and education, and the visibility of the site. Those figures, and our analysis of them, are what we discuss with you after the report is delivered.
