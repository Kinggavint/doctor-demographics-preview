Why does location matter for an orthodontic practice?

An orthodontic practice depends on children and teens who can reach the office, and on the general dentists nearby who may refer them. Since 1983, Doctor Demographics has written 10,000+ reports for dentists and specialists, including orthodontists, who are choosing or evaluating a location. Every report is hand-written for the specialty and includes a live 20-minute follow-up call.

The age band that matters most here is narrower than for a general dental office: children ages 7 to 17, within a 10 to 15 minute drive, and whether that group is growing. Young families and new housing, household income, and schools close to the site all feed that same question. So does competition, measured as orthodontists per child in the area, and the presence of general dentists who can refer.

The questions we hear most often from orthodontists are: how many kids live within driving distance, are there enough general dentists around to refer, and is this area growing with young families or getting older. Those answers have to come from the site or the counties you ask us to study. This page states the factors we use. It does not invent a minimum child count or a standard referral number.

Which demographic factors matter most for orthodontics?

  • Children ages 7 to 17 within a 10 to 15 minute drive, and whether that group is growing. This is the core demand question for an orthodontic site.
  • Young families and new housing. These help show whether the area is adding the households that will need orthodontic care, or drifting older.
  • Household income. Income helps describe whether families in the drive-time area can support the practice you want to operate.
  • General dentists nearby who can refer. Referral relationships start with whether those dentists are actually around the site.
  • Orthodontists per child in the area. We look at how many orthodontists already serve the children who live there.
  • Schools close to the site. Nearby schools are part of how families move through the area during the day and are one of the factors we include for this specialty.

Frequently asked questions

Do you write reports specifically for orthodontic practices?

Yes. Every report is written for the client's specialty. How long does a report take?

Standard turnaround is 5 to 7 business days for every report, including the Competition Report. If you need it sooner, a rush option is available; we can usually turn a report around in 24 to 48 hours when that option is selected.

Is there a call after the report?

Yes. Every report is hand-written and includes a live 20-minute follow-up call to go over the findings. When we email the finished report, we include a booking link to schedule that call. You can also reply to that delivery email and we will set a time. Spouses, partners and consultants are welcome on the call.

How many kids live within driving distance?

In the report we look at the number of children ages 7 to 17 within a 10 to 15 minute drive of the site, and whether that group is growing. We do not put a single target number on this page, because the right figure depends on the site and on the rest of the local picture, including income, referring dentists and other orthodontists. We will walk through the counts for your site on the follow-up call.

Are there enough general dentists around to refer to me?

Referral access is one of the factors we study. We look at general dentists nearby who can refer, together with the number of children in drive time, schools close to the site, and how many orthodontists there already are per child in the area. A Site Viability Report is the usual way to see that picture around a specific address.

Is this area growing with young families, or getting older?

That is one of the questions an orthodontic report is written to answer. We look at young families and new housing, and at whether the number of children ages 7 to 17 within a 10 to 15 minute drive is growing. The report will describe what is happening in the area you ask us to study, including when the area is getting older rather than younger.
